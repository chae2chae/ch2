02 Methods

Core Idea, Pros, And Cons

All three are pure odometry systems. They do not include loop closure, so long-range drift remains a shared limitation.

FAST-LIO

v1 · 2021

Core idea: Solve the iEKF Kalman gain in state dimension instead of measurement dimension, making updates fast even with high-rate LiDAR measurements.

Pros

  • Lightweight and computationally cheap, useful for embedded or small platforms
  • Tightly coupled and fast for its time

Cons

  • Depends on feature extraction, so it is weaker in vegetation, tunnels, and other feature-poor scenes
  • Uses a regular kd-tree, so map updates are more expensive
  • FAST-LIO2 is better for most practical use cases

FAST-LIO2

2022 · default

Core idea: Remove feature extraction and use raw point direct registration with ikd-Tree for incremental insert, delete, and rebalance operations.

Pros

  • Feature-free design is robust in unstructured or degraded environments
  • Fast map updates through ikd-Tree, so real-time performance is strong
  • Good accuracy-speed tradeoff and the largest practical ecosystem

Cons

  • Frame-based update with IMU as input remains vulnerable to IMU saturation and extremely aggressive motion
  • Not designed for kHz-level odometry output
  • Still pure odometry: drift and lack of loop closure remain

Point-LIO

2023 · aggressive motion

Core idea: Include angular velocity and acceleration in the state as random-walk variables. The IMU becomes a measurement of those states rather than an input to propagation.

Pros

  • Prediction is decoupled from IMU integration, enabling point-by-point updates and ~kHz output
  • Robustness to IMU saturation is the main selling point; saturated values can be rejected or down-weighted
  • Good fit for high-bandwidth control loops

Cons

  • Process noise Q becomes a sensitive new parameter, so tuning strongly affects performance
  • For benign motion, the benefit may be marginal
  • Requires saturation thresholds and noise characterization, adding calibration burden

04 Backend

For SLAM Or Navigation

Premise: these three systems are front-end odometry methods. They do not provide loop closure or global optimization.

Drift accumulates over long trajectories, and revisiting the same place will not automatically correct the map. Add the backend that matches the application.

LiDAR + IMU
raw sensor stream
FAST-LIO2 / Point-LIO
front-end odometry · odom→base_link · high rate
A · Full SLAMAdd loop detection, such as Scan Context, plus a pose graph with GTSAM/iSAM2 to obtain a globally consistent map. Examples include FAST-LIO-SLAM or factor-graph-based LIO-SAM.
B · NavigationUse relocalization against a prior map to publish map→odom. Example: FAST-LIO-LOCALIZATION. Keep mapping mode and localization mode separate.
Map Conversion
occupancy grid (2D) · OctoMap (3D) · elevation map
Nav2 / move_base
global plan → local plan → dynamic obstacle avoidance → cmd_vel

TF Tree

map→ odom→ base_link→ sensor frames
odom → base_link : published at high rate by the LIO front-end, such as FAST-LIO2 or Point-LIO
map → odom : published by localization or loop-closure backend as the drift correction term

In short, LIO owns odom→base_link, while the backend owns global consistency through map→odom. Keeping this split clean makes the navigation stack much easier to reason about.
